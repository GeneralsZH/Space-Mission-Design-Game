# Orbital Mission Lab

**Lunar Ice Mapper: design it, fly it, explain the outcome.**

Orbital Mission Lab turns lunar mission design into an interactive engineering game. Students start with a budget and an empty spacecraft, purchase and assemble subsystems, configure orbital altitudes and inclination, and balance mass, propellant, electrical power, storage and communications. They then watch their spacecraft launch, orbit the Moon and return simulated data. A persistent geometric survey trail and planned-event countdown connect operations to the scene. Underpowered or poorly configured designs show consequences, including damaged-cell generation loss, followed by an engineering debrief that supports revision.

A shared lunar two-body model connects the rocket equation, Kepler period, vis-viva speed, eclipse duration and sensor access to the 3D flight. New missions use deterministic engineering criteria rather than random success percentages. Students can compare the same hardware in different orbits and export a standalone report.

The science explorer uses genuine NASA LRO/LROC annual south-pole illumination observations, with source pixels, dates, units, archive links and checksums. It clearly separates observed illumination from simulated spacecraft access and never presents either as an ice-abundance measurement. NASA SVS LRO-derived imagery supplies the 3D Moon's appearance, with separate visualization credits.

This competition upgrade extends an existing React/TypeScript prototype. New contributions include connected lunar dynamics, deterministic crisis rules, reproducible NASA data extraction, coverage comparisons, reporting and automated verification. Hardware specifications and operational scenarios remain simplified educational assumptions, documented in the README.
